Altis road network index

Road indexing runs as part of normal map indexing. Locations use adaptive footprints and spatial compound merging. Compatible facilities merge using original-core distances and footprint area, without object-count caps. Initial category evidence minimums still apply. The map header reports the counts for the loaded build.

Explore

Scroll to zoom, drag to pan, or search a town, site ID, road ID or X,Y coordinates. Click a road to inspect its connectivity or highlight its network. Select a site marker to show numbered road inlets. The Dorida inlets shortcut shows eight crossings; select a number and use Zoom to inlet to inspect it. Dorida fallback selects a power site with a 36 m strong approach. Green dashed lines are gaps up to 150 m; purple dashed lines are secondary approaches through 500 m. Select a baseline connection to see road travel in teal, then Frame connection. Share or bookmark the address to return to the same view.

What the map shows

33,733 usable road sections form 60 networks under ordinary engine connectivity. There are 40 bridge sections. Colours show road class or connected network. Dashed red lines show extended-search evidence, excluded from the routing graph. Geometric road crossings alone never add a connection.

Yellow numbered markers show roads crossing the saved site boundary. Each crossing is retained, including both sides of through-roads and closely spaced entrances. Arrows point inward. Blue markers indicate a crossing interpolated across a gap between ordinary-connected road sections. Sites without boundary inlets receive nearby-road candidates where available. Sites with no candidate within 500 m remain in the index with unknown accessibility. Gates, fences and vehicle traversal remain untested. A geometric inlet is not a verified compound entrance.

Spatial merge review

The central airport now owns the southwest military compound. The northern base remains separate because its support objects extend beyond the 250 m whole-component bound. Geometric overlap alone does not imply ownership. Sites retain their original component IDs and secondary roles for review.

Routes and uncertainty

Routing anchors use only source-supported ordinary links. The export contains baseline attachment connections and a compact graph retaining all junctions and alternatives. Fallback-only anchors no longer stop baseline discovery. Genuine boundary inlets still stop discovery except at its origin; nodes carrying both kinds stop as well. The Dorida–Chalkeia direct connection is now present alongside both towns’ connections to the nearby power site. An objective never becomes a free transit shortcut. Road distance, endpoint off-road gaps and anchor connectors are separate measurements. Teal paths describe the strategic graph, not executable vehicle movement. Dashed gaps have not been checked for water, fences, slopes or usable entrances.

Map detail and offline use

Detailed topographic tiles load online from Arma3Map; terrain copyright Bohemia Interactive. Native detail ends at zoom 6; higher zoom enlarges it. The bundled island overview and road network remain available offline. Viewing never launches Arma.

Rebuild

Run IVCS/Locations/Indexing/run.ps1 to build locations, road attachments, routing data and this viewer together. Use -InventoryPath with the saved object inventory to skip an object rescan, or -RescanRoads to refresh the native road capture. No LLM is required.

The graph is physical evidence, not guaranteed vehicle routing. Runtime ownership, danger, damage and vehicle costs are intentionally separate.
